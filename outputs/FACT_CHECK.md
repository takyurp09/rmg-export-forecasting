# Fact check: EU duty assumptions for S2

Checked 24 Oct 2026. All URLs below were accessed on that date. EUR-Lex, TARIC and Access2Markets pages did not render for us, so the regulation text was read from the "as adopted" copy on legislation.gov.uk. That copy is the original EU text; the EU amended it after 2020, but not in the articles below as far as we found.

## 1. EU MFN duty on cotton apparel is 12%

Verdict: confirmed for CN 6109 10 00 and 6205 20 00 from secondary sources only. The other lines (6110 20, 6203 42, 6204 62) were not checked line by line. Under 6110 20 and 6203 42 some subheadings may carry lower rates.

- celna24.pl, CN 6205 20 page (TARIC mirror): states the standard EU duty for men's cotton shirts is 12%. https://celna24.pl/customs-tariff/620520
- celna24.pl, CN 6109 10 page: shows a 0-12% range across origins. https://celna24.pl/customs-tariff/610910
- The legal source is Annex I to Reg. (EEC) 2658/87 as replaced for 2026 by Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2025/1926 (OJ L, 31 Oct 2025). We could not open it. https://taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu/news/commission-publishes-2026-version-combined-nomenclature-2025-10-31_en

## 2. Standard GSP duty on apparel is 9.6%

Verdict: the arithmetic is right, and 8.3% would be wrong. Article 7(2) of Reg. (EU) 978/2012 cuts ad valorem duties on sensitive goods by 3.5 percentage points. Its second subparagraph says that for GSP sections S-11a and S-11b (textiles and clothing) the cut is 20% instead. So 12% x 0.8 = 9.6%.
https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2012/978/article/7/adopted

The bigger problem is that 9.6% would probably never apply. Article 8 and Annex VI point 2 suspend preferences in sections S-11a and S-11b for a standard GSP country whose share of GSP imports in that section exceeds 14.5% over three years. Bangladesh is the largest apparel supplier in the scheme, so it would almost certainly cross that line and pay 12%. We did not compute the share; this rests on Bangladesh's size relative to the other standard GSP beneficiaries.
https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2012/978/annex/VI/adopted

The regulation was extended to 31 Dec 2027 by Reg. (EU) 2023/2663, which changed the year in Article 43(3). The EBA arrangement has no expiry date. Rules for after 2027 are not settled in anything we found.
https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/2663/oj/eng
https://trade.ec.europa.eu/access-to-markets/pt/news/eu-scheme-generalised-tariff-preferences-gsp-extended-until-31-december-2027

Change made: the 9.6% stays, but S2b is now described as unlikely, and S2 (12%) as the likely case without GSP+, not an upper bound.

## 3. UN decision on deferring graduation to 2029

Verdict: no decision yet. ECOSOC passed the request to the General Assembly. The Second Committee was due to take it up on 7 Oct 2026. On 19 Oct the Commerce Minister said a decision was expected within October. We found no adopted resolution. The legal date is still 24 Nov 2026 (UNGA res. 76/8).

## 4. EBA ends three years after graduation

Verdict: confirmed, and the repo cited the right paragraph. Article 17(2) says a country leaves EBA "following a transitional period of three years" counted from the entry into force of the delegated act that removes it. The Commission normally times that act to the UN graduation date. Article 17(1) only sets eligibility.
https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2012/978/article/17/adopted

## S3 stress test

Drop it, or at least take it out of the headline. S1 is a demand shock that could happen next year. S2 cannot start before about Nov 2029. Adding them treats two events years apart as one year's loss, and the sum tells a reader nothing that S1 and S2 do not already say separately. If a combined case is wanted, it should be built on a post-2029 baseline, and that baseline does not exist in this repo.
